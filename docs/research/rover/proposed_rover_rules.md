# PROPOSAL: driving-mode flight rules for LARA

**Status: proposal by the team, not NASA rules.** NASA has no flight rules for a crewed Mars rover. Each row names where its number comes from and how far to trust it. IDs such as D21 or R27 point to the evidence tables in `rover_guidelines_research.md`.

- **applies_to** says where the number comes from:
  - Moon = Apollo LRV or Artemis
  - Mars robotic rover = Curiosity or Perseverance
  - Mars crewed study = an assumption in a NASA design study
  - assumption = our own proposal; nothing is published
- **Confidence**:
  - High = the same number appears in several NASA Mars crewed studies
  - Medium = a confirmed NASA number, but taken from the Moon, from a robot, or from a single study
  - Low = our assumption, or a number we derived
- Every source number below was confirmed at source unless marked otherwise.

## What these defaults mean for 90–500 km sites (derived, not citable)

Energy per km for the default preset:

| Terrain  | Nominal     | Bad terrain (k = 2) |
| -------- | ----------- | ------------------- |
| Flat     | 1.84 kWh/km | 3.38 kWh/km         |
| 10° up   | 3.68        | 6.80                |
| 10° down | 0.38        | 1.63                |
| 20° up   | 5.49        | >100 (stalls)       |

**On one charge:**

- The 200 kWh battery has 160 kWh above the 20% floor.
- Keeping a 20 km reserve (37 kWh) leaves 123 kWh for the trip.
- That gives about 33 km one way if the return is nominal, or about 24 km if the return is costed as bad terrain.
- These figures leave out the hotel load while parked, about 46 kWh per sol.
- The 60 kWh option reaches only about 5 km one way. MAT's own figure is 12 km out and back (C12) because it uses η = 1 and no path factor. The margins stack, so the team should tune η and k deliberately.

**Over 14 sols (rule 28, using rule 29's rates):**

- Solar only: 196 km on the odometer, about 75 km one way.
- With 2 fission units: 644 km on the odometer, about 248 km one way, which rule 26 caps at 200 km.

| Site distance | Round-trip odometer (×1.3) | Sols, solar only | Sols, 2 fission units | Rules hit                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 90 km         | 234 km                     | 16.7 → no-go     | 5.1                   | Buddy rover needed (rule 23) |
| 150 km        | 390 km                     | 27.9 → no-go     | 8.5 → caution         | Caution at >100 km (rule 25) |
| 250 km        | 650 km                     | 46 → no-go       | 14.1 → no-go          | No-go at >200 km (rule 26)   |
| 500 km        | 1,300 km                   | 93 → no-go       | 28 → no-go            | No-go at >200 km (rule 26)   |

**Bottom line:** under NASA's published assumptions, only the nearest sites (about 90–150 km) are reachable, and only with two rovers plus deployed power or recharge stops. Sites at 250–500 km need supply caches or infrastructure that no NASA study sizes.

## Open choices for the team

1. **η and k.** These drive the whole battery result, and both are our assumptions. Show a sensitivity band (nominal vs bad) rather than a single number.
2. **Slope limits on MOLA data.** Rule 2 is a heuristic. If CTX DTMs cover the first leg (near 46 N, 188–189 E), use rule 1 there.
3. **Field recharge.** Rule 29 rates come from one 2015 study. Model recharge as an explicit per-sol input if the demo needs it.
